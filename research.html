Research

My interests are broad. I am interested in mathematical and computational structures underlying physical, chemical, and engineered systems, especially when ideas from mathematics, physics, computation, and engineering meet in a concrete scientific problem.

My current paper trail is centered on many-body theory and electronic structure. The organizing question is how to move from mathematical formulations to computational approximations and then to useful models for electronic, molecular, and materials systems. Beyond this core direction, I also enjoy thinking about problems in computational mathematics, mathematical physics, quantum science, machine learning, materials science, semiconductors, devices, circuits, chips, and engineering systems more broadly.

Recent research program

Much of my recent work moves from mathematical formulation to computational realization and effective description.

  • Mathematical foundations. I study the variational and convex-analytic structure of quantum many-body and electronic-structure problems. Central themes include energy functionals, ensemble formulations, duality, and derivative and response structures, with density-functional theory and Kohn–Sham theory as guiding examples.
  • Computational realization. I ask how the objects defined by these theories can be reconstructed, approximated, and used in computation, including inverse Kohn–Sham maps, exchange-correlation information, response functions, Green's functions, tensor approximations, optimization algorithms, and machine-learning parametrizations.
  • Effective descriptions and applications. I use these structures to build reduced or approximate models for electronic, molecular, and materials systems, including quantum embedding, force-field perspectives, quantum simulation, molecular modeling, and AI for science.

The papers page gives a more concrete view of these directions.
