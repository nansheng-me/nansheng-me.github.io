Research

My interests are broad, but they are unified by a common theme: the mathematical and computational structure of complex physical, chemical, and engineered systems. My background spans mathematics, physics, chemistry, scientific computing, and machine learning, and I like problems where rigorous structure, physical modeling, and computation meet.

My current paper trail is centered on many-body theory and electronic structure. At the same time, I keep a wide technical horizon, including AI, statistics, generative modeling, high-performance computing, materials science, semiconductors, circuits, chips, and engineering systems more broadly.

Recent research program

Much of my recent work moves from mathematical formulation to computational realization and effective description.

  • Mathematical foundations. I study the variational and convex-analytic structure of quantum many-body and electronic-structure problems. Central themes include energy functionals, ensemble formulations, duality, and derivative and response structures, with density-functional theory and Kohn–Sham theory as guiding examples.
  • Computational realization. I ask how the objects defined by these theories can be reconstructed, approximated, differentiated, and used in computation. Examples include inverse Kohn–Sham reconstruction, exchange-correlation information, response functions, Green's functions, tensor approximations, optimization algorithms, and machine-learning parametrizations.
  • Effective descriptions and applications. I use these structures to build reduced or approximate descriptions of electronic, molecular, and materials systems, including quantum embedding, force-field perspectives, quantum simulation, molecular modeling, and AI for science.

Selected research threads

The following threads give a more concrete sense of the range of problems and methods that I think about.

  • Density-functional theory and convex analysis. Exact formulations of DFT, Lieb-type duality, ensemble variational principles, Kohn–Sham constructions, inverse Kohn–Sham problems, exchange-correlation functionals, and response theory.
  • Quantum many-body methods and Green's functions. Quantum embedding, quantum defect embedding theory, dynamical mean-field theory, low-rank Green's-function representations, correlated electronic systems, and quantum simulation.
  • Scientific computing and high-dimensional approximation. Tensor methods, functional hierarchical representations, high-dimensional Gibbs distributions, Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equations, numerical approximation, optimization, scientific software, and parallel or high-performance computing.
  • Machine learning, statistics, and AI for science. Statistical and machine-learning methods for scientific problems, including scientific foundation models, large language models, diffusion and score-based generative models, uncertainty, robustness, machine-learning quantum chemistry, AI-assisted scientific computing, and scientific coding agents.
  • Materials, molecules, and engineering systems. Electronic-structure, molecular, and materials modeling; force-field viewpoints; semiconductors, devices, circuits, chips, and engineering systems more broadly. I see these directions as connected by the same interaction between physical modeling, computation, and design.
