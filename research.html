Research

Overview

My research is broadly concerned with the mathematical and computational structure of complex physical, chemical, and engineered systems. I am especially interested in problems where rigorous mathematical structure, physical modeling, and computation meet in a concrete scientific setting.

Recent work

My recent work spans several related directions in mathematical physics, electronic structure, scientific computing, and AI for science. A common theme is to identify useful mathematical structure in physical or chemical systems and turn it into computable models or algorithms.

  • Variational electronic-structure theory. One line of my recent work studies density-functional theory and Kohn–Sham-type constructions from a variational and convex-analytic viewpoint. This includes exact formulations of DFT, ensemble variational principles, inverse Kohn–Sham problems, exchange-correlation functionals, and response theory.
  • Computational many-body methods. Another line of work concerns numerical representations and algorithms for many-body problems. This includes Green's-function representations, dynamical mean-field calculations, quantum embedding methods, quantum simulation, and reduced descriptions of correlated electronic systems.
  • High-dimensional scientific computing. I am also interested in computational methods for high-dimensional scientific problems, including tensor methods, functional hierarchical representations, optimization, high-dimensional Gibbs distributions, and Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equations.
  • AI for science and molecular/materials modeling. My recent industry and collaborative work also connects to machine learning for molecular and materials science, including robust machine-learning quantum chemistry methods, force-field perspectives, scientific coding agents, and data-informed models that retain physical structure.

Broader interests

Beyond my current paper trail, I also enjoy thinking about statistics, diffusion and generative models, high-performance computing, quantum computing, mathematical and theoretical physics, condensed-matter and high-energy theory, materials science, semiconductors, devices, circuits, chips, and engineering systems more broadly.

The papers page gives a publication-oriented view of these directions.
